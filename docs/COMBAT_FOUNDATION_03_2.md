# Combat Foundation 03.2 — health, impact damage and defeat

This milestone extends the uncommitted 03.1 melee foundation while retaining all earlier 06D work. It is local on `feature/milestone-05-animation`, HEAD `2735828`; nothing was staged or committed.

## Play

Open `res://scenes/world/AshenfallDwarfPlayableDemo.tscn` in Godot 4.7.2 Mono and press **F6**. WASD and left-click/hold on terrain still move the dwarf. Click the Fallen Guardian to issue one approach and one axe attack. A valid impact removes **25 HP** from its initial **100 HP**; four valid hits defeat it. Space issues another one-shot attack against a selected living target, or a practice swing without a target. After defeat, clicks on the former enemy position become ordinary ground clicks, and the dwarf can walk through it.

## Separation and timing

- `HealthComponent` owns bounded hit points and emits `HealthChanged` and a single terminal `Died`. It has no actor, input, UI or animation dependency. Its maximum is exported and the Guardian sets it to 100.
- `MeleeTarget` optionally references health and is targetable only while that health is alive. The target marker remains a separate child of the collision actor.
- `PlayerMeleeController` keeps the 03.1 one-order/one-attack state machine and 1.70 s logical attack. Each attack receives a monotonic sequence number and snapshots its committed target. The new `AttackImpact` signal fires once at `ImpactFraction = 0.94 / 1.70` of the snapped cycle duration. At 60 physics ticks/s the first impact occurred 55–59 frames after the start in the focused test. Ground clicks or selection changes during the swing cannot redirect its committed target.
- `MeleeDamageController` observes that signal. It consumes the sequence before validation, so duplicate signals and previously missed strikes never gain an extra hit. At impact it checks the current sequence/committed target, alive and targetable health, horizontal melee range, height and first-hit physics line of sight, then applies its exported 25 damage. The controller is callable independently of mouse input and the visual animation. A future authoritative host still needs stable network IDs and command validation; neither is implemented here.
- `DwarfPlayerPresentation` remains an observer of `AttackStarted`; the approved Guard B, Combat Walk and Impact C resources are unchanged. No animation event applies damage. The logical duration continues to determine the visual clip playback rate.
- `EnemyDeathController` reacts to `Died`: it clears targetability, invokes the terminal `EnemyController.Defeat()` state, and defers disabling the body shape and collision layers/masks. `Defeated` was appended to the existing enum to preserve earlier state values. Enemy navigation, detection and `AttackExecuted` stop, its player target is cleared, and the node remains alive. The world navigation bake uses static environment geometry and is not rebuilt for a defeated dynamic enemy.
- `EnemyAnimationController` stops the active clip and tilts the visual root forward over 0.65 s. This is a minimal fallback because there is no approved death clip. It does not alter approved animation resources or gameplay transforms.
- `WorldHealthBar` observes health only and displays one noninteractive billboard quad above the Guardian. Its exported `HealthyThreshold` (0.50), `CriticalThreshold` (0.25), `HealthyColor`, `WoundedColor`, `CriticalColor`, and `BackgroundColor` are presentation settings, independent of health and damage. Above 50% the fill is green; above 25% through 50% orange; above 0% through 25% red; at zero only the dark background remains. One persistent opaque texture contains both the colored left segment and dark right segment. A health change edits pixels in a persistent `Image` and calls `ImageTexture.Update`; no geometry, material or texture is created during the update. Each enemy owns one material and texture, so their colors are independent. The quad has no collision and never intercepts clicks.

## Persistent bar rendering diagnosis and correction

Manual play rejected the earlier two-quad bar. Runtime `SceneTree` inspection of the actual `AshenfallDwarfPlayableDemo` found **one** `/root/AshenfallDwarfPlayableDemo/FallenGuardian/HealthBar` and two child meshes, `Background` and `Fill`; no duplicate bar was instantiated. Both were billboarded independently. The fill's old update moved its center in **world X** by `-0.14`, `-0.28`, and `-0.42` units at 75/50/25 HP. With the isometric camera, its projected center moved about **5.9 px left and 4.0 px up per hit** while the background center stayed fixed. The depth also changed. The transparent background and opaque fill used different render queues. This produced the apparent stationary black bar plus a displaced colored bar. The previous private-QuadMesh width correction did not fix this transform mismatch.

The correction renders background and fill regions in a **single fixed-size quad and one opaque billboard material**. There is no per-segment transform or transparent overlay. The quad remains at the same world and screen position at every HP value, and the texture's colored segment shortens from left to right.

## Limits and manual review

The death fallback is a simple forward collapse, not a crafted death animation. At gameplay distance the corpse is small and can read as a dark shape; inspect it manually before approving the art. Defeat freezes the actor's physics at its current location, which is appropriate for the grounded Guardian in this prototype but would need a different policy for airborne actors. There is no hit reaction, player health, damage from the enemy, hit effects, loot, experience, respawn or networking. Attack sequences are unique within the lifetime of one controller; they are not network IDs.

Manual checks: run F6, approach and strike the Guardian four times, inspect the bar at 100/75/50/25/0, watch its death during a possible enemy windup, click the corpse, walk across its former collider, and confirm terrain click/hold, camera and gate movement still feel unchanged. Also inspect the collapse from the gameplay camera. Automated tests performed these mechanics, but a person has not yet approved the final visual result.
